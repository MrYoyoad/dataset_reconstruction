\title{Why Linearization Accuracy and Adapter Leakage \emph{Decouple}:\\
the gate-range account}
\author{Yoad Oxman}
\date{2026-08-13 (rev.\ 4 --- the bridge-connection duality; walks back the $\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$ claim)}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Two quantities that ``ought'' to move together do not: making the activation smoother \emph{lowers}
the NTK/anchor linearization error, yet on the LoRA/adapter path the \emph{kinked} ReLU --- the worst
linearizer --- \emph{leaks the most} instance-level information. This note pins the mechanism.
Reconstruction inverts a factorization $\dW_1 = G\,\diag(c)\,X^\top$ whose left factor is the
\emph{gate matrix} $M_{ki}=\sigma'(\ip{w_k}{x_i})$. Leakage is governed by $\rank(M)$: a neuron carries
per-sample information only if its gate value \emph{varies across the samples}. That variation is the
\emph{range of $\sigma'$ over the sample pre-activations} --- generated by the curvature $\sigma''$, but
only the $\sigma''$ \emph{in the band the data occupies}, and made rich by $\sigma''$ \emph{varying}
across that band. Linearization error is separately governed by $\sigma''$ along the training
trajectory. Both are readouts of $\sigma''$ with opposite sign of benefit, so no activation optimizes
both: smooth units give an accurate model but a nearly rank-one (blurred) gate matrix (matching the
measured Softplus $\texttt{eff\_rank}=1$); ReLU gives binary, maximally distinct gates (high rank,
high leakage) at the cost of linearizability. A \emph{second}, independent ceiling then applies: even
distinct features must be carried through a rank-$\rho$ channel, and if that rank falls far below the
data manifold's intrinsic dimension $k$ (so $\rho(m+d)\ll kN$), the best possible reconstruction of the
unobserved directions is exactly zero --- regardless of activation. A cheap gate-matrix diagnostic makes
the feature ceiling falsifiable; the measurements (\S\ref{sec:measured}) \textbf{confirm} it (eff\_rank$(M)$
predicts the leakage ordering; Softplus-$\beta$ is a monotone dial) and reveal a \textbf{duality} --- the
collinearity that makes a smooth unit \emph{least} leaky to direct inversion makes its gradient
\emph{most} decodable by the bridge (softplus: $0.997$). They also \emph{walk back} the linearisation
side: no simple fine-grained $\sigma''$ law (neither $\sigma''$ nor $\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$) predicts
the relative lin-error; only the coarse kinked-vs-smooth (Dirac) split is robust.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
A victim fine-tunes a public $\theta_0$ on private data $\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ by $T$ steps of
gradient descent on the BCE loss, producing $\theta_T$; the attacker observes $\dW=\theta_T-\theta_0$
(full FT) or its low-rank LoRA factors and tries to recover $\{x_i\}$. The network is
\begin{equation}
  f(x;\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^m v_j\,\sigma(\ip{w_j}{x}),\qquad \theta=(W,v),\ W\in\R^{m\times d}.
\end{equation}
Reconstruction linearizes $f$ about an anchor $\theta_{\mathrm a}=(1-\alpha)\theta_0+\alpha\theta_T$ and
matches $\dW$ to a weighted sum of per-sample gradient features $\phi(x):=\nabla_\theta f(x;\theta_{\mathrm a})$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
  \dW\approx\sum_{i=1}^N c_i\,\phi(x_i),\qquad c_i=-\eta\,\ell'\!\big(f(x_i;\theta_{\mathrm a}),y_i\big).
\end{equation}

\section{The observation is a factorization through the gate matrix}
For the first layer, $\partial f/\partial w_j = v_j\,\sigma'(\ip{w_j}{x})\,x$, so
$\phi_W(x)=g(x)\,x^\top$ is rank one, with the \emph{gating vector}
$g(x)=\big(v_1\sigma'(z_1),\dots,v_m\sigma'(z_m)\big)^\top$, $z_k:=\ip{w_k}{x}$. Substituting,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
  \dW_1=\sum_{i=1}^N c_i\,g(x_i)\,x_i^\top=G\,\diag(c)\,X^\top,\qquad
  G=\diag(v)\,M,\quad \boxed{\,M_{ki}=\sigma'(\ip{w_k}{x_i})\,}.
\end{equation}
Here $X=[x_1,\dots,x_N]\in\R^{d\times N}$ and $M\in\R^{m\times N}$ is the \textbf{gate matrix}: entry
$(k,i)$ is how strongly neuron $k$ gates sample $i$. Since $\diag(v)$ is invertible ($v_k\neq0$),
$\rank(G)=\rank(M)$.

\begin{proposition}[Identifiability $=$ rank of the gate matrix]\label{prop:ident}
The factorization \eqref{eq:factor} determines the individual $x_i$ (up to the known scales $c_i$)
\emph{iff $\rank(M)=N$}. If $\rank(M)=r<N$, only $r$ combinations of the $c_ix_i$ are observable and the
other $N-r$ directions are a null space of indistinguishable recombinations --- the \textbf{superposition
problem}. Leakage is therefore set by $\rank(M)$ (equivalently the effective rank of $\dW_1$), not by
how accurate the linear model is.
\end{proposition}

\section{The two quantities we care about}
\paragraph{(A) Linearization error} --- the project's headline anchor metric, the function-space Taylor
residual along the observed displacement $\delta=\theta_T-\theta_{\mathrm a}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{lin}}:=\norm{f(x;\theta_{\mathrm a}+\delta)-\big[f(x;\theta_{\mathrm a})+\phi(x)^\top\delta\big]}
  =\tfrac12\,\delta^\top\nabla^2_\theta f\,\delta+o(\norm{\delta}^2).
\end{equation}
Forward-model accuracy.

\paragraph{(B) Leakage} --- by Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}, $\rank(M)$. Inverse-problem conditioning.
These are different objects; the rest of the note is why the \emph{same} activation property drives
them oppositely.

\section{What makes $\rank(M)$ large: the range of $\sigma'$ over the samples}
A row of $M$ (neuron $k$) is $\big(\sigma'(z_k^1),\dots,\sigma'(z_k^N)\big)$. It carries no per-sample
information if it is \emph{constant} across $i$; it contributes an independent, informative direction
only if the gate value \emph{varies} over the samples. So $\rank(M)$ counts neurons whose gate spans a
range across the data.

\begin{lemma}[Gate range $\;\approx\;$ local curvature $\times$ sample spread]\label{lem:range}
For neuron $k$, by the mean value theorem,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:range}
  \max_i\sigma'(z_k^i)-\min_i\sigma'(z_k^i)\;\approx\;\underbrace{\sigma''(\bar z_k)}_{\text{local gain}}\;
  \underbrace{\big(\max_i z_k^i-\min_i z_k^i\big)}_{\text{pre-activation spread of the samples}} .
\end{equation}
Hence the gate range --- and thus whether neuron $k$ contributes to $\rank(M)$ --- is set by
$\sigma''$ \textbf{in the band the data occupies}, times how far apart the samples' pre-activations are.
A large $\sigma''$ sitting where no sample lands does nothing.
\end{lemma}

\noindent The magnitude of $\sigma''$ is \emph{not} the whole story --- three regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] \textbf{Linear $\sigma$ ($\sigma''\equiv0$):} $\sigma'\equiv$ const $\Rightarrow$
  $M=a\,\bm1\bm1^\top\Rightarrow\rank(M)=1$. Zero range, total superposition, no leakage. (Airtight endpoint.)
  \item[(ii)] \textbf{Quadratic $\sigma$ ($\sigma''\equiv c\neq0$, constant):} $\sigma'$ affine, so
  \begin{equation}
    M=a\,\bm1\bm1^\top+c\,WX\quad\Rightarrow\quad \rank(M)\le 1+\rank(WX).
  \end{equation}
  Rank \emph{does} appear --- but the gate is merely a \emph{linear} readout of the data, a degenerate
  kind of separability (the net is essentially linear). So constant curvature already gives rank; its
  magnitude only scales the conditioning.
  \item[(iii)] \textbf{Genuine nonlinearity ($\sigma''$ \emph{varies} across the range):} GELU/SiLU/Softplus
  and, in the limit, ReLU. Neurons whose transition sits at different locations place \emph{different
  subsets} of samples in their steep zone, so $M$ becomes a nonlinear, heterogeneous, near-binary code
  --- higher and more \emph{robust} rank than the linear $WX$. ReLU is the extreme: $\sigma''$ a Dirac at
  each kink, $\sigma'\in\{0,1\}$, $M$ binary with maximally distinct rows.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Why the ReLU limit is the maximum, via total variation]
For ReLU $\sigma''$ is zero a.e.\ and a Dirac at the kink; the exact identity is still
$g_k(x_i)-g_k(x_j)=v_k\!\int_{z_k^j}^{z_k^i}\sigma''=v_k\cdot(\text{0 if same side of the kink, }\pm1\text{ if straddling})$.
So ReLU's gate spans its \emph{full} range $0\!\to\!1$ (samples on opposite sides of a kink) --- the
largest possible per-sample distinction, hence the largest $\rank(M)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Linearization residual: absolute $\propto\sigma''$, relative $\propto\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$]\label{lem:lin}
The Hessian block is $\partial^2 f/\partial w_k\partial w_k^\top=v_k\,\sigma''(z_k)\,x x^\top$, so
$\nabla^2_\theta f$ is linear in $\{\sigma''(z_k)\}$ and the \emph{absolute} residual is
$O\!\big(|\sigma''|\,\norm{\delta}^2\big)$. The headline anchor metric, however, is the \emph{relative}
residual (normalised by $\norm{\Delta\Phi}\approx\norm{\nabla\Phi\cdot\delta}$), so at matched
$\norm{\delta}$ it tracks $\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$ --- curvature \emph{relative to} gradient
magnitude --- not $\sigma''$ alone. \textbf{Empirically, however, even this is too clean}
(\S\ref{sec:measured}): the coarse kinked-vs-smooth split survives (there $\sigma''$ jumps by orders of
magnitude via the Dirac kink), but \emph{within} the smooth family neither $\sigma''$ nor
$\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$ predicts the relative lin-error (Pearson $+0.11$ and $+0.04$) --- GELU
linearises better than Softplus, but no simple curvature ratio captures the ordering. This lemma holds
only at the coarse level.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[The curvature tradeoff]\label{prop:tradeoff}
Linearization accuracy wants $\sigma'$ to vary \emph{little} as $\theta$ (hence $z$) moves ---
small $\sigma''$ along the trajectory. Leakage wants $\sigma'$ to vary \emph{a lot} \emph{across the
samples} --- a wide gate range \eqref{eq:range}, generated by $\sigma''$ in the data band. Both are
``$\sigma'$ changing with $z$'', i.e.\ $\sigma''$, so
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{lin}}\ \uparrow\ \text{with }|\sigma''|\ \text{(trajectory)},
  \qquad
  \rank(M)\ \uparrow\ \text{with the range of }\sigma'\text{ over the samples (}\propto\sigma''\text{ in the data band)} .
\end{equation}
No activation maximizes leakage and minimizes linearization error at once; the attainable pairs lie on a
frontier. The property that makes the Taylor expansion accurate --- $\sigma'$ slowly varying --- is
exactly what makes the gate insensitive to \emph{which} $x$ produced it. Smoothness buys an accurate
model of a blurred signal.
\end{proposition}

\section{The capacity ceiling: gate rank is necessary, not sufficient}\label{sec:capacity}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ident} makes $\rank(M)=N$ \emph{necessary} to separate the samples, but not
\emph{sufficient} to recover them. The observed update is itself rank-limited (LoRA: $\dW=BA$,
$\rank\le r$) and must encode $X\in\R^{d\times N}$. This is a second bottleneck, set by the
\emph{data dimension} rather than the activation.

Count degrees of freedom. A rank-$\rho$ update carries $\approx\rho(m+d)$ free parameters, where the
effective rank composes the three limits
\begin{equation}
  \rho=\min\big(\rank(M),\ r,\ N\big)\quad(\text{gate rank},\ \text{LoRA rank},\ \text{sample count}).
\end{equation}
If the private data lie on a manifold of intrinsic dimension $k$, specifying $\{x_i\}$ given the
manifold costs $\approx kN$ parameters. Recovery is information-theoretically possible only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity}
  \rho\,(m+d)\ \gtrsim\ kN,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \rho\ \gtrsim\ \frac{kN}{m+d}.
\end{equation}
When $\rho(m+d)\ll kN$ --- small rank, many samples, or a \emph{high-dimensional data manifold} --- the
observation is a compressed sketch that cannot resolve the data. The minimum-error reconstruction is the
projection of $X$ onto an $\approx\rho(m+d)/N$-dimensional subspace, and its energy in the complementary
$\big(k-\rho(m+d)/N\big)$ directions is \textbf{irrecoverable --- the best approximation there is exactly
zero}. For a rich manifold ($k$ large) the recovered fraction $\to \rho(m+d)/(kN)\to0$: the best-case
reconstruction approaches the null map, \emph{independent of activation, gate rank, or optimizer}.

\begin{proposition}[Two independent ceilings]\label{prop:ceilings}
Leakage is bounded by the smaller of a \emph{feature} ceiling and a \emph{capacity} ceiling,
\begin{equation}
  \text{leakage}\ \lesssim\ \min\Big(\ \underbrace{\rank(M)}_{\text{activation / curvature}}\ ,\ \
  \underbrace{\tfrac{\rho\,(m+d)}{kN}}_{\text{rank vs.\ data dimension}}\ \Big).
\end{equation}
The activation decides whether distinct per-sample features \emph{exist}; the rank-vs-dimension budget
decides whether they can be \emph{carried}. Both must clear, and the tighter one binds.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textbf{Consequences.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Richer data lowers leakage at fixed rank.} MNIST digits have $k\sim10$--$20$; natural
  images (faces, flowers) $k\sim10^2$--$10^3$. Larger $k$ tightens \eqref{eq:capacity}, so the same
  adapter leaks less --- the reason the flowers-native track must re-examine $r$ and $N$
  (the information-density argument).
  \item \emph{A generative prior raises the ceiling by lowering $k$.} A diffusion / SDS prior restricts
  $x$ to the image manifold, cutting the \emph{effective} intrinsic dimension $k$; this relaxes
  \eqref{eq:capacity} and can lift leakage above the raw-pixel ceiling --- the theoretical justification
  for the generative-prior direction.
  \item \emph{Why ``$0.95$-cosine gradient $\ne$ image'' (GB-Phase~2).} Even a faithfully recovered
  gradient must still clear \eqref{eq:capacity} to yield pixels; a high-fidelity but low-rank measurement
  of high-$k$ data is capacity-limited no matter how good the gradient is.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Softplus-$\beta$ knob dials the frontier}
For $\sigma_\beta(z)=\beta^{-1}\log(1+e^{\beta z})$, $\sigma_\beta'(z)=s(\beta z)$ (logistic) and
$\sigma_\beta''(z)=\beta\,s(\beta z)(1-s(\beta z))$, with transition width $\sim 1/\beta$ and
$\max_z\sigma_\beta''=\beta/4$. For a fixed pre-activation spread $\Delta z$, the gate range a neuron
spans is $\approx s(\beta z^{\max})-s(\beta z^{\min})$, which is negligible when $\beta\Delta z\ll1$
(flat transition, samples unresolved) and saturates toward $1$ when $\beta\Delta z\gg1$ (sharp
transition, samples straddle it). So $\beta$ dials both leakage \emph{and} $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lin}}$:
the sharp, falsifiable prediction is that along \texttt{SOFTPLUS\_BETA\_SWEEP} both the gate-matrix rank
(hence leakage) and the linearization error increase with $\beta$, with the leakage rise concentrated
near $\beta\Delta z\sim1$ (transition width matched to the sample spread). This is the theory-cleanest
experiment in the activation study.

\section{Consequences and consistency with the data}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Effective-rank collapse (measured), correctly explained.} Prop.~\ref{prop:ident}:
  $\rank(\dW_1)\approx\rank(M)$. The rescore found Softplus $\texttt{eff\_rank}=1$. The reason is
  \emph{not} that $\sigma''$ is globally zero (it is not) --- it is that over the \emph{sample band}
  $\sigma'=s(\beta z)$ barely varies (the $x_i$ cluster in a nearly-flat / uniformly-sloped region), so
  $M\approx a\bm1\bm1^\top$, rank one, entangled. ReLU spans the full gate range $\Rightarrow$ high rank
  $\Rightarrow$ the leakage you measured ($+0.12\!\to\!+0.23$ on LoRA).
  \item \textbf{Why LoRA amplifies.} The adapter records $P\dW$ for a rank-$r$ projection $P$. The
  near-constant rows of a smooth $M$ collapse under $P$; the distinct (binary) rows of a ReLU $M$
  survive. So the identifiability gap widens under the low-rank bottleneck --- why relu$>$smooth is
  sharp on LoRA but muddier on full FT.
  \item \textbf{The anchor $\alpha$ is activation-dependent (observed).} Moving $\theta_{\mathrm a}$
  toward $\theta_T$ shifts the pre-activations $z_k$, sliding the samples onto or off each neuron's
  steep region --- i.e.\ changing the gate range \eqref{eq:range}. For a smooth unit this can
  \emph{restore} gate variation (the anchor rescues GELU/SiLU: LoRA margin $+0.03\!\to\!+0.14$); a kink
  is already at maximal range (ReLU best at $\alpha{=}0$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Falsifiable predictions and a cheap test}\label{sec:test}
Both need only a forward pass and one backward pass at $\theta_{\mathrm a}$ (no $50$k-epoch extraction).
Form the gate matrix $M_{ki}=\sigma'(\ip{w_k}{x_i})$ per activation and report:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Gate-matrix rank vs.\ leakage.} the effective rank of $M$; the per-neuron gate variance
  $\Var_i\,\sigma'(z_k^i)$ (mean over $k$); the mean pairwise column cosine of $G$. Prediction:
  \emph{ReLU} $\Rightarrow$ high $\rank(M)$, large gate variance, low cosine (separable); \emph{smooth}
  $\Rightarrow$ $\rank(M)\approx1$, near-zero gate variance, high cosine (entangled). $\rank(M)$ tracks
  the leakage margins; $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lin}}$ tracks $\sigma''$ along the trajectory.
  \item \textbf{Monotone $\beta$.} along Softplus-$\beta$, both $\rank(M)$ and $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{lin}}$
  increase with $\beta$; the leakage rise concentrates near $\beta\Delta z\sim1$.
\end{enumerate}
These were run (\S\ref{sec:measured}) and the dissociation collapses to one law: \emph{a wide gate range
across the samples buys leakage; a flat gate along the trajectory buys linearization accuracy; both are
$\sigma''$ (the relative lin-error being $\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$), read on different arguments.} That
is the project's open identifiability question (Q-A: when is $\dW\mapsto\{x_i\}$ well posed?) answered in
terms of the model.

\section{Measured results (2026-08)}\label{sec:measured}
The tests of \S\ref{sec:test} were run (MNIST MLP, $\theta_0$ anchor, seed 42; a forward $+$ one
backward pass, no extraction). Summary:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Feature ceiling --- CONFIRMED.} eff\_rank$(M)$ at $N{=}10$:
  relu $6.37\approx$ leaky $6.33 \gg$ \textbf{selu $3.39$} $>$ gelu $2.91 >$ silu $2.34 >$ softplus
  $1.73 >$ tanh $1.59 >$ sigmoid $1.19$ --- kinked $\sim\!4\times$ smooth, and it \emph{predicts} the
  independently-measured leakage ordering, including the surprise that the C$^0$ kink \emph{selu}
  out-leaks every smooth C$^\infty$ unit. cos$_M$: softplus $0.997$ (collinear, rank$\approx1$) vs relu
  $0.81$; on the full gradient features cos$_\Phi$: softplus $0.85$ (entangled) vs relu/gelu $\sim0.39$.
  \item \textbf{Softplus-$\beta$ is a clean monotone dial.} $\beta\!:0.5\to50$ gives eff\_rank$(M)$
  $1.40\to5.30$, gate range $0.037\to0.555$, $\overline{|\sigma''|}$ $0.125\to3.26$, cos$_M$
  $0.999\to0.869$ --- a one-parameter traversal of the frontier toward the ReLU limit
  ($6.37/0.585/\text{Dirac}/0.810$).
  \item \textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:lin} --- only the coarse split holds; no fine $\sigma''$ law (rev.4
  walk-back).} At \emph{matched} weight-change, kinked $\gg$ smooth linearisation error is robust
  (relu $0.576$/leaky $0.562$ vs smooth $0.02$--$0.13$): smoothness$\to$linearisation is \emph{real},
  not a $\norm{\delta}$ artifact. But \emph{within} the smooth family neither $\sigma''$ nor the
  proposed $\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$ correction predicts the relative lin-error ---
  Pearson$(\text{lin\_err},\sigma'')=+0.11$ vs Pearson$(\text{lin\_err},\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi})=+0.04$
  (\emph{worse}). gelu still linearises better than softplus, but no simple curvature ratio orders the
  smooth family. \textbf{The rev.3 $\sigma''/\norm{\nabla\Phi}$ claim is withdrawn}; Lemma~\ref{lem:lin}
  holds only coarsely (kink vs.\ no kink).
  \item \textbf{Bridge connection --- a DUALITY between the two attacks (new).} Training the R2F decoder
  $f_\phi:(A,B)\to\nabla_W L$ per activation (hidden layer, $r{=}8$; projection ceiling $\approx0.087$),
  the decoded full-cosine is \textbf{softplus $0.997$} (stable; the $>\!0.9$ milestone met on a
  \emph{single-sample} gradient) $\gg$ softplus\_b50 $0.773 >$ relu $0.750 >$ gelu $0.685 >$ silu $0.664$.
  The \emph{same} collinearity that makes softplus the \emph{worst} for direct inversion
  ($\text{cos}_M=0.997$, $\rank(M)\approx1$: entangled samples, least leakage) makes its gradient the
  \emph{most decodable} by the bridge. \textbf{The two attacks have opposite activation preferences} ---
  and GELU (the deployment C$^\infty$ unit) is the \emph{least} decodable, so the bridge is hardest on
  the deployment-realistic activation. \textbf{But end-to-end (GB-Phase 2) the bridge FAILS to recover
  images:} the $0.997$ is on the \emph{hidden} layer, which does not contain $x$; the \emph{input} layer
  --- the only one whose gradient is $g_{\mathrm{err}}\,x^\top$ --- decodes only to $0.637$ (softplus),
  because its input is the raw, diverse images rather than the clustered hidden activations. Decoding
  that and taking the top right singular vector gives SSIM $\approx0.02$ (a vague blob). So the layer the
  bridge can decode is not the layer that holds the image, and the image layer decodes too poorly to
  yield pixels --- the bridge's high cosine does not translate to a reconstruction; direct inversion
  (full $\dW$) remains the attack that works.
  \item \textbf{LoRA amplification --- CONFIRMED (rank sweep).} At $N{=}10$: \emph{relu is rank-robust}
  (control margin $+0.05$, ssim\_norm $0.57$ already at $r{=}1$, flat across $r$) --- its distinct
  binary gates survive even a rank-one projection; \emph{gelu is rank-climbing} ($+0.01$/$0.44$ at
  $r{=}1$, rising to a plateau by $r\!\approx\!16$) --- its collinear gates collapse under low rank and
  need more of it. The gap is largest at low rank.
  \item \textbf{Capacity ceiling / $N$-collapse --- CONFIRMED.} gelu, $r{=}8$: the instance-leakage
  margin decays $+0.033\to+0.023\to+0.012\to+0.006$ and retrieval falls to chance as $N:10\to64$ ---
  leakage per sample $\to0$ as $kN$ eats the $\rho(m+d)$ budget. (ssim\_norm stays $\sim0.48$: shared
  background, not instance information.)
  \item \textbf{High-$k$ test on the flowers-native MLP ($d{=}3072$) --- N-collapse replicates, the
  \emph{specific} high-$k$ prediction FAILS (honest).} The N-collapse and rank-climbing both replicate on
  this harder dataset with its own $\theta_0$. But the intrinsic-dim proxy came out \emph{backwards}:
  eff\_rank$(X)$ at $N{=}64$ is MNIST $\mathbf{46.3} > $ flowers64 $\mathbf{39.7} > $ flowers32 $37.3$
  --- \emph{even} $d{=}12288$ flowers64 is \emph{lower} effective-rank than MNIST (downsampled natural
  images collapse to a few global ``blob-on-green'' modes vs.\ ten spread-out digit shapes), so $kN$
  does \emph{not} tighten faster and the margin decay matches MNIST. The capacity ceiling did not bind
  distinctly earlier; the only clear difference is far lower reconstruction quality (ssim\_norm $0.09$
  vs.\ $0.48$), i.e.\ the harder ambient space, not the capacity term. \textbf{The high-$k$ item is
  closed negatively:} downsampled flowers are not high-$k$ at all; isolating the capacity ceiling needs
  genuinely high-frequency (full-resolution) data.
\end{itemize}

\section{Caveats}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Local, data-dependent.} ``Leakage $\propto\sigma''$'' is really ``$\propto$ the range of
  $\sigma'$ over the \emph{sample} pre-activations''; a curvature the data does not visit is inert. The
  operative object is the gate matrix $M$, not $\sigma''$ in the abstract.
  \item \emph{Leading order / smooth case.} \eqref{eq:range} and Lemma~\ref{lem:lin} are first order for
  one hidden layer; ReLU enters as the total-variation / Dirac limit. Depth adds products of gates but
  preserves the sign.
  \item \emph{Homogeneity.} KKT/max-margin recovery assumes positively-homogeneous nets; smooth $\sigma$
  break this, so the NTK/gradient-feature account here is the relevant one (it needs no homogeneity). The
  factorization \eqref{eq:factor} is exact regardless.
  \item \emph{Empirical status.} Single-seed, $N\in\{2,10\}$, $T=10$, MNIST, oracle coefficients ---
  directional. The gate-matrix test (\S\ref{sec:test}) is what turns this note from hypothesis into
  measurement; the capacity ceiling (\S\ref{sec:capacity}) is a separate, data-dimension bound.
\end{itemize}
