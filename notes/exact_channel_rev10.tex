\section{The exact channel: from the quotient to the full release}
\label{sec:exact-channel}

\paragraph{What changed since Revision 9.}
Revision 9 proposed, as Primitive~3, a \emph{conditional} route past the
quotient ceiling: a curvature/KKT gradient-mixture equation requiring a known
metric $K$ and a remainder bound. That route is retained as the
recipe-\emph{unknown} fallback (\S\ref{sec:opens}), but it is no longer the
primary escape. The primary escape is \emph{exact}: under the same hypotheses
that give the quotient, the whole release is a deterministic function of the
private data plus $rN$ nuisance numbers, and that function can be inverted.
The exact channel has its own capacity, $k < m + r - N$, which sits exactly
$m$ above the quotient's $k < r - N$; the capacity is a dimension count whose
strict form is now derived (the softmax simplex constraint,
Proposition~\ref{prop:simplex}), it is measured to be sharp to one unit of
$k$, and past it — and only past it — the release is reproduced at the floor
by a point other than the truth. That is a boundary of \emph{exact
identifiability}; on the present evidence it is \emph{not} a boundary of
leakage, because the other point sits within $0.2$--$1.2\%$ of the true
images (\S\ref{sec:measured}). The quotient's ceiling $r-N$ is therefore a
fact about one channel, not a bound on the leakage.

\subsection{Setting and the theorem chain we stand on}
\label{sec:setting}

A frozen layer $W_0\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ receives private representations
$H=[h_1,\dots,h_N]\in\mathbb R^{n\times N}$, $\operatorname{rank}H=N$. LoRA
adds $W_{\mathrm{eff}}=W_0+sBA$ with $A\in\mathbb R^{r\times n}$,
$B\in\mathbb R^{m\times r}$; standard initialisation is $B_0=0$, $A_0$ random.
The attacker receives the trained $(A_T,B_T)$ only. Let
$U=\operatorname{orth}(H)$, $V=\operatorname{orth}(H^\perp)$ and write
\[
  X := A_0U\in\mathbb R^{r\times N},\qquad Y := A_0V\in\mathbb R^{r\times(n-N)} .
\]

\begin{theorem}[closure of the factor dynamics — Rev~9 Lemma~2]\label{thm:A}
\textnormal{[THEOREM]} Under (A1)--(A3) below, for every $t$ there exist
$P_t\in\mathbb R^{m\times N}$, $\widetilde M_t\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}$, $c_t$ with
\[
  B_t = P_t\,(A_0H)^{\!\top},\qquad A_t = A_0\bigl(c_tI + H\widetilde M_tH^{\!\top}\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[the quotient — Rev~9 Thm~1, Cor~1]\label{thm:B}
\textnormal{[THEOREM]} $C := P_{\mathrm{row}(B_T)^\perp}A_T = c_T\,P_{(A_0H)^\perp}A_0$,
hence $CH=0$, $\ker C=\mathcal H\oplus\ker A_0$ and
$\operatorname{rank}C = r-N$ exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[the quotient family has a ceiling — Rev~9 Prop~5]\label{prop:F}
\textnormal{[THEOREM]} Eliminating only $A_0,P_T,\widetilde M_T,c_T$ from the
terminal invariants imposes nothing on $H$ beyond $CH=0$ and the rank events.
The quotient channel supplies exactly $r-N$ linear equations per private
representation, and no rearrangement of the same invariants adds a row.
\end{proposition}

Theorems~\ref{thm:A}--\ref{thm:B} and the pull-back statements (Rev~9 Thm~3,
Prop~4: $\operatorname{rank}(C|_T)=\min(r-N,\,k-d_\parallel)$ a.s., and local
isolation when $\sigma_{\min}(L_x)>0$) are unchanged. Everything below is new.

\subsection{Primitive 3, exact: the release is a function of the data and $rN$ numbers}
\label{sec:reduction}

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} The exact channel uses the quotient's hypotheses
(A1)--(A4) — not (A5) — plus one that Revision~9 did not state, (A6).
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item[(A1)] \emph{Fixed inputs.} The same $H$ reaches the adapter at every
  step: no augmentation, no LoRA dropout, no trainable upstream block. This is
  the load-bearing hypothesis; violating it is a structural collapse (the
  invariant subspace becomes the union of per-step spans, $\mathrm{row}(B_T)\neq
  \mathrm{col}(X)$, $CH\neq0$, $\operatorname{rank}C\to0$), not a degradation.
\item[(A2)] $B_0=0$; $A_0$ drawn from an absolutely continuous law.
\item[(A3)] \emph{Linear optimiser.} The update is a scalar linear filter of
  current and past gradients and iterates (SGD, classical momentum, scalar
  decoupled shrinkage), or a matrix map $M\mapsto p(MM^{\!\top})M$.
  Coordinatewise Adam normalisation violates this (\S\ref{sec:opens}).
\item[(A4)] $\operatorname{rank}P_T=N$ — stated as a \emph{hypothesis} of the
  certificate, not a corollary. It needs $m\ge N$ and $N$ independent
  accumulated residual trajectories; it fails for duplicated, already-fit or
  degenerate-label examples, and when it fails $C$ is \emph{contaminated}
  ($CH\neq0$), not merely weaker.
\item[(A5)] $r\ge N+1$ — a hypothesis of the \emph{quotient} only (it is what
  gives the certificate its $r-N\ge1$ rows). Theorem~\ref{thm:G} does not use it.
\item[(A6)] \emph{Canonical basis.} The orthonormal basis $U_c(w)$ of a
  candidate span is computed by a QR with $\operatorname{diag}R>0$. A bare QR
  flips a whole basis column when a candidate feature crosses zero, so the map
  below is not even continuous without this convention. It is a hypothesis of
  the theorem, not an implementation detail.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[reduction: the release depends on $A_0$ only through $X$]\label{thm:G}
\textnormal{[THEOREM]} Under (A1)--(A4) and (A6), the entire trajectory
$(A_t,B_t,D_t,P_t,\widetilde M_t)_{t\le T}$ is a measurable function of
$(X,H,W_0,\text{recipe})$ alone; $Y$ is never touched. Explicitly
\[
  A_T = X\,\Omega\,U^{\!\top} + c_T\,Y\,V^{\!\top},\qquad
  B_T = P_T X^{\!\top},\qquad
  \Omega = c_TI_N + R_H\widetilde M_TR_H^{\!\top},
\]
so that, with the private data written in manifold coordinates $w$
($H=\varphi(\psi(w))$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F}
  \bigl(B_T,\;A_TU\bigr) \;=\; F(w,X),\qquad X\in\mathbb R^{r\times N}.
\end{equation}
The release is a deterministic function of the candidate data and $rN$
nuisance numbers. (A5) is not used: the trajectory depends on $A_0$ only through
$X=A_0U$ whatever the relative sizes, and the normal form goes through with
$\operatorname{rank}X=\min(r,N)$; in particular $N\ge r$ — where the
certificate is the zero operator — is \emph{permitted} by the reduction. It is
untested here: every cell run has $N\le14<r=16$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, $B_T$ and $A_TU$ are functions of $A_0H=A_0UR_H=XR_H$;
the coefficient recurrences for $P_t,\widetilde M_t$ (Rev~9, proof of Lemma~2)
are driven by $D_tH^{\!\top}$ and $A_0H$, hence by $X$; the block $Y=A_0V$ enters
$A_T$ only as the additive term $c_TYV^{\!\top}$, which is orthogonal to $U$ and
carries no data. The identity for $A_T$ follows by writing $A_0 = XU^{\!\top}+YV^{\!\top}$.
\end{proof}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:B}'s insight used forward instead of cancelled: the
quotient deletes the data-bearing part of $A_T$ to expose an $A_0$-free
equation; the reduction keeps it and shows the unknown initialisation enters
only through $rN$ numbers, which are then solved for. The attacker does not observe $A_TU$ — $U$ is the true span basis and is
unknown — but $A_T$, and forms $A_TU_c(w)$ for a candidate $w$. The object
actually inverted is therefore the \emph{residual map}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
  \rho(w,X) := \Bigl(\tfrac{B_{\mathrm{sim}}(w,X)-B_T}{\|B_T\|},\;\;\tfrac{\Xi_{\mathrm{sim}}(w,X)-A_TU_c(w)}{\|A_T\|}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
whose second block has a non-zero $w$-derivative through the moving target
$U_c(w)$. The blocks are normalised by the \emph{fixed} released norms —
$\|A_T\|$, deliberately not $\|A_TU_c(w)\|$, which moves with $w$ and could be
shrunk by the optimiser; this scales the spectrum of $D\rho$, not its rank.
Every Jacobian below ($D\rho$, written $DF$ for short) includes the
moving-target term. The attack is $\min_{w,X}\|\rho(w,X)\|^2$ by Levenberg--Marquardt, with
the gradient obtained by back-propagation through the unrolled recipe. \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]}: fed the
true $(w,X)$, the simulator reproduces the released factors to relative error
$7.4\times10^{-16}$--$8.5\times10^{-16}$ (jobs 395496, 396214--21).

\subsection{The equation count, stated as a cap — and kept separate from sufficiency}
\label{sec:counting}

The number that matters is not ``how many entries the release has'' but the
dimension of the \emph{image} of $F$. Two facts bound it.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item $A_TU_c\in\mathbb R^{r\times N}$ is $rN$ numbers; it can supply at most
  $rN$ independent constraints, whatever it carries.
\item $B_T=P_TX^{\!\top}$ has rank $N$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}) and, under a
  softmax head, zero column sums (Proposition~\ref{prop:simplex}); it lies on
  the manifold of rank-$N$ matrices in $\mathbf 1^\perp\otimes\mathbb R^r$, of
  dimension $N\bigl((m-1)+r-N\bigr)$; it can supply at most that many.
\end{itemize}
Unknowns: $Nk$ (data, $k$ manifold coordinates per image) $+\,rN$ (the
nuisance $X$). Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
  \underbrace{N\bigl((m-1)+r-N\bigr)+rN}_{\text{cap on the release's degrees of freedom}}
  \;\ge\;
  \underbrace{Nk+rN}_{\text{unknowns}}
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\; k\le (m-1)+r-N
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \boxed{\;k < m + r - N\;}
\end{equation}
(the $rN$ cancels exactly; the strict inequality is the integer form of
$k\le(m-1)+r-N$). The deficit of one per image comes from the head, and it
is a theorem, not a fit:
\begin{proposition}[the simplex constraint]\label{prop:simplex}
\textnormal{[THEOREM]} Under a softmax cross-entropy head, $B_0=0$ and (A3),
$\mathbf 1^{\!\top}B_t=0$ for every $t$. Hence
$B_T\in\mathbf 1^\perp\otimes\mathbb R^r$, and the locus of rank-$N$ matrices
available to it has dimension $N((m-1)+r-N)=N(m+r-N)-N$: the plain rank-$N$
cap carries a deficit of exactly $N$, one per image.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The softmax error columns sum to zero, $\mathbf 1^{\!\top}D_t=0$ (each column
is $p_i-y_i$ with both on the simplex). The $B$-gradient is
$D_t(A_tH)^{\!\top}$, so $\mathbf 1^{\!\top}\nabla_B=0$; a scalar linear
filter of such gradients preserves the identity, and so does the matrix map
$M\mapsto p(MM^{\!\top})M=M\,p(M^{\!\top}M)$ — so the constraint holds on the
whole (A3) closure class, Muon-type orthogonalisers included, and the strict
form is not an SGD artefact; induction from $B_0=0$ gives
$\mathbf 1^{\!\top}B_t=0$. Identify $\mathbf 1^\perp\otimes\mathbb R^r$ with
$\mathbb R^{(m-1)\times r}$ by an isometry; rank-$N$ matrices there form a
manifold of dimension $N((m-1)+r-N)$.
\end{proof}
\textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} two independent ways (job 479684):
$\|\mathbf 1^{\!\top}B_T\|/\|B_T\|=3\times10^{-16}$--$1.9\times10^{-15}$
across every SGD release on disk, all shapes, all $T$, all seeds; and the
measured $B$-block rank at SGD $n=96,k=12,N=8$ is $216=N((m-1)+r-N)$
\emph{exactly}, where the plain cap would be $224$. Before this derivation the
strict form rested on two cells collapsing at equality; it now rests on the
head. Compare the quotient's condition $k<r-N$: the exact channel's capacity
is \emph{exactly $m$ higher per image}.

\paragraph{Both halves of the cap are measured, and attained.}
\textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} (job 479684) The per-block rank of $DF$ at the true
$(w,X)$ measures the two supplies separately, rather than inferring them from
where recovery fails:

The $B$-block rank equals its cap in both $n=96$ rows, and the cap is
regime-specific: SGD attains the simplex-constrained $N((m-1)+r-N)=216$, Adam
attains the plain $N(m+r-N)=224$, because Adam's coordinatewise normalisation
does not preserve zero column sums (\S\ref{sec:opens}, item~3). What looked
like slack — $216$ against $224$ — is Proposition~\ref{prop:simplex}, tight.
The $A$-block rank equals its cap in every row ($rN$ under SGD, $rn$ under
Adam). The inequality~\eqref{eq:count} is therefore a measured equality of
supplies, not only a boundary inferred from failures. (In the small SGD cell
the $B$-block alone already carries all $88$ columns, so its cap is not
binding there.)

\medskip
Two statements must be kept apart at every mention, because only the first can
ever be measured directly:
\begin{description}\itemsep2pt
\item[Necessary (counting).] The block bounds hold \emph{everywhere}, not
  only at the truth: the $A$-block is $r\times N$ by construction, and the
  $B$-block has rank $N$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}) with zero column sums
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:simplex}), so
  $\operatorname{rank}D\rho\le N((m-1)+r-N)+rN$ identically. If $k\ge m+r-N$
  this cap is below the number of unknowns. For real-analytic $\rho$ the set
  where the rank is not maximal is contained in a proper analytic subvariety
  — closed, with empty interior and Lebesgue measure zero — and off it the
  constant-rank theorem makes the fibre of $\rho$ through $(w,X)$ a
  submanifold of dimension $\ge Nk+rN-\bigl(N((m-1)+r-N)+rN\bigr)>0$.
  Through almost every point there is therefore a \emph{continuum} of
  release-consistent points \textnormal{[THEOREM]}. Two cautions. Rank
  deficiency \emph{at a point} would not suffice ($f(x)=x^2$ has $f'(0)=0$
  yet an isolated fibre); the statement is a.e.\ and rests on the bounds
  holding everywhere. And a positive-dimensional fibre says nothing about how
  far it extends: \emph{non-isolation is not distance}. Direct check
  \textnormal{[VERIFIED†, extent pending]} (job 482338; $N=8$, $k=32$, $40$
  numerically null directions): stepping along the $\sigma_{\min}$ singular
  direction and retracting onto the fibre by a few LM steps keeps the residual
  at $\sim\!10^{-30}$ at every one of $25$ continuation steps; the image error
  rises (locally non-monotone) to a maximum of $1.43\times10^{-2}$ at the
  floor — past the study's own $10^{-2}$ recovery tolerance — and sits in the
  $1.0$--$1.4\times10^{-2}$ band over the last five steps of that path. At the
  line itself ($N=14$, $k=22$, $14$ null directions) sixteen steps reach
  $3.5\times10^{-2}$ and are \emph{still rising} as this is written (job
  482338 running; final extent to follow). In
  neither cell is the extent bounded by the run. \emph{The caveat belongs next
  to the numbers:} each walks one null direction out of forty (resp.\
  fourteen) — the continuation follows the least-constrained singular
  direction, re-aiming as the fibre curves, but it is a single path through
  the set — so these bound the fibres' extent from \emph{below}; the pause of
  the $k=32$ path in the $1.0$--$1.4\%$ band is a property of that path, not
  of the fibre, and the at-line path shows none. (Quote the maximum image error at the floor, not a distance along the
  path: arc length lives in the joint $(w,X)$ space, where the $rN$ nuisance
  coordinates advance it without moving the image.) The below-the-line control
  ($N=8$, $k=26$), where the traversal must be \emph{obstructed}, is
  \textnormal{[EMPIRICAL — pending, same job]}; if the continuation walks as
  freely below the line, this paragraph is withdrawn.
\item[Generically sufficient (Theorem~\ref{thm:H}).] If $k<m+r-N$ then $DF$
  has full column rank at almost every $(w,X)$, so the truth is locally
  isolated. \textnormal{[THEOREM, given one witness]}.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[the count is a cap, not a mechanism]\label{rem:attribution}
It is tempting to read~\eqref{eq:count} as ``the $A$-block pins $X$ and the
$B$-block carries the data''. That reading is false, and it failed twice in
one day: the regression target $A_TU_c(w)$ moves with the candidate, so the
$A$-block equation \emph{slaves} $X$ to $w$ rather than consuming $rN$
equations to determine it; and $\Omega$ is data-dependent, so the $A$-block
carries data as well. Neither fact changes~\eqref{eq:count}, because the cap
is a statement about block \emph{sizes and ranks}, not about which block pins
which unknown. Present the count only as a cap. A second trap, hit three times
in one day: writing the map with its target fixed at the true $A_TU$ instead
of the candidate-dependent $A_TU_c(w)$. The moving target is what couples $X$
to $w$; omit it and every count that follows is wrong.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Generic local identifiability of the exact channel}
\label{sec:thmH}

\begin{theorem}[generic local identifiability]\label{thm:H}
\textnormal{[THEOREM, modulo one explicit witness]} Under (A1)--(A4) and (A6),
$\rho$ is real-analytic on the open set $\{\operatorname{rank}H_c=N\}$
(non-empty in every cell tested; density would require $\varphi\circ\psi$ to
be generically of full rank, verified only numerically)
($\tanh$ generator and encoder, softmax cross-entropy, a finite composition of
analytic update maps, and the canonical QR, which is analytic on the
full-column-rank set). Suppose $k<m+r-N$. Then either $DF$ has full column
rank at almost every $(w,X)$, or it is rank-deficient everywhere. A single
point of full column rank therefore establishes: for almost every
initialisation and almost every private data set, the true $(w,X)$ is locally
isolated among release-consistent points — with no dependence on $r-N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof]
The maximal minors of $D\rho$ are real-analytic functions on
$\{\operatorname{rank}H_c=N\}$, which is connected because it is the complement
of a proper analytic subset of a connected real-analytic manifold;
their common zero set is an analytic subvariety, which is either the whole
domain or of Lebesgue measure zero (identity theorem). Full column rank at one
point excludes the first case. Local isolation at a full-rank point is the
inverse-function theorem, exactly as in Rev~9 Prop~8; the proof pattern is
that of Rev~9 Thm~3.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The witness is explicit, at $T=1$.} Because $B_0=0$ forces
$\nabla_A L_0=0$, one step gives $A_1=A_0$, hence $\Xi_{\mathrm{sim}}=X$ and
\[
  \rho_{T=1}(w,X) \;=\; \Bigl(-\eta\,D_0(w)\,R_c(w)^{\!\top}X^{\!\top}-B_T,\;\;\; X-A_TU_c(w)\Bigr),
\]
a closed form with no unrolling. The second block is \emph{not} independent of
$w$: $\partial\rho_2/\partial w=-A_T\,\partial U_c/\partial w$, and it is
exactly this term that makes the $T=1$ budget $m+r-N$ rather than $m$
(writing the target as the fixed $A_TU$ instead predicts $k\le m$, which the
measured full rank at $k=24$ refutes). Its Jacobian is measured full column
rank at $(N,k)=(8,16),(8,20),(8,24)$ \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} (job 479587). A
symbolic verification of this Jacobian at one point is the remaining step to
make Theorem~\ref{thm:H} unconditional at $T=1$; extending the a.e.\ claim
to $T>1$ rides either on a witness per $T$ or on the measured
$T$-independence of $\sigma_{\min}(DF)$ (next paragraph).

\paragraph{A refuted conjecture, recorded because of \emph{why} it failed.}
It was conjectured that the capacity is $T$-dependent — $k\le m$ at $T=1$
(``the $A$-block hands $X$ over for free'') rising to $k<m+r-N$ at $T\ge2$.
Measured \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} (job 479587, $N=8$): $\sigma_{\min}(DF)$ at
the truth is $7.26\times10^{-4},\,1.21\times10^{-4},\,3.15\times10^{-5}$ at
$k=16,20,24$ for \emph{both} $T=1$ and $T=2$ (agreement to three significant
figures), and collapses at $k=28$ for both ($3.3\times10^{-19}$,
$1.3\times10^{-20}$). The capacity is $T$-independent and set by geometry,
and the conjecture failed for precisely the reason in
Remark~\ref{rem:attribution}. The witness at $T=1$ is a consequence, not a
degeneracy.

intact — identifiable, not yet solved; six restarts reach the floor,
$9.5\times10^{-31}$, with error $2.8\times10^{-12}$. The local-vs-tractable
distinction, exhibited and then closed, in one cell.)

The failure \emph{signature} changes at the line and nowhere else. Below it,
every failure observed in the programme has a residual above the floor — a
search failure — and no alias has ever been seen. Above it, the solver
reproduces the release to $\sim\!9\times10^{-31}$ from a point that is not
the truth: exact identifiability is lost, cleanly separated from conditioning
failures by the residual alone (Figure~\ref{fig:quadrant}).

\emph{But the other point is close.} Past-line image errors are
$1.7\times10^{-3}$ ($N=4$), $2.6$--$9.3\times10^{-3}$ ($N=8$),
$6.4\times10^{-3}$ ($N=14$), $1.2\times10^{-2}$ ($N=12$); in $8$ of the $11$
past-line cells every image is inside the study's own $10^{-2}$ recovery
tolerance, and a $0.3\%$ relative error is a visually identical image. So the
capacity law is a boundary of \emph{exact identifiability} and, on this
evidence, \emph{not a boundary of leakage}: past it the attack still returns
every image to sub-percent accuracy. The fibre is, however, traversable: the
continuation of \S\ref{sec:counting} moves the image along it to $1.4\%$
(past the line) and $3.5\%$ (at the line) error with the residual at the
floor throughout, along one null direction of forty and of fourteen
respectively, in neither case bounded by the run. On the paths measured the
alternative solutions are still recognisable reconstructions; whether the
fibre reaches unrecognisable points is not established, and one cell is still
rising at the end of its run. The capacity boundary marks where \emph{exact}
recovery stops; whether it marks anything about recognisable recovery is the
open question that decides whether the line has any privacy meaning at all.
The $49$-cell grid
$N,k\in\{2,\dots,14\}$ (jobs 396214--21, 456630) lies entirely inside the
capacity region and recovers in every cell, crossing the quotient line
$k=r-N$ throughout (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}).

The recipe is the price of the extra $m$. The quotient reads only the
trajectory-invariant $r-N$ rows of $A_T$ and uses $B_T$ solely for its row
space; the exact channel reads all of $B_T$ and pays by simulating the
trajectory. Consequently ``the adapter exposes only $r-N$ independent rows''
is a true statement about the quotient and a false one about the leakage.

\subsection{Open mathematics}
\label{sec:opens}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Local is not global.} Theorem~\ref{thm:H} isolates the truth in
  a neighbourhood. Global injectivity of $F$ on the manifold — no distant
  alias with identical release — is open, exactly as Rev~9 states for the
  certificate. Empirically no alias has appeared below the line
  \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]}; that is evidence, not a theorem.
\item \textbf{The recipe as domain parameters.} The exact channel assumes the
  recipe (optimiser family, $\eta$, $T$, $s$, decay) is known — the standard
  assumption of the KKT-reconstruction line. Unknown recipe parameters enlarge
  the domain of $F$ by $p$ scalars; the cap becomes
  $N(m+r-N)+rN\ge Nk+rN+p$, still a dimension statement. Three claims are
  \textnormal{[EMPIRICAL — running, jobs step14+]}: (i) misspecification is
  self-detecting (a wrong recipe cannot reach the floor); (ii) a handful of
  recipe scalars can be fitted jointly with $(w,X)$; (iii) non-identifiable
  combinations such as $\eta T$ (small-step regime) are harmless. The
  certificate is the recipe-\emph{free} channel, at the price of the cap
  $r-N$; the curvature/KKT mixture of Rev~9 \S3.6--3.8 remains the
  recipe-\emph{unknown} approximate route.
\item \textbf{Adam breaks (A3), not identifiability.} Coordinatewise
  normalisation breaks the closure of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, so
  Theorem~\ref{thm:G}'s reduction fails and the nuisance is the full
  $A_0\in\mathbb R^{r\times n}$: the unknowns become $Nk+rn$ and the
  equations $mr+rn$. \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} (jobs 466915, 467622): the
  Jacobian shapes show it directly — SGD $88=Nk+rN$ columns against Adam
  $536=Nk+rn$ at $n=32$; $224$ against $1632$ at $n=96$ — and
  $DF$ remains full column rank at the truth in every Adam cell. In one line
  with the per-block ranks of job 479684: a $B$-block supply of $224$ against
  SGD's $216$, twelve times the nuisance ($1536$ against $128$), the same
  data unknowns. The $224$ is not incidental: Adam's entrywise division by
  $\sqrt{v}+\epsilon$ does not preserve zero column sums, and
  \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} $\|\mathbf 1^{\!\top}B_T\|/\|B_T\|=2.6$--$2.7$ for
  every Adam release against $10^{-15}$ under SGD. Adam saturates the
  \emph{plain} cap $N(m+r-N)$ because it breaks the simplex constraint that
  binds SGD to the smaller one (Proposition~\ref{prop:simplex}).
  \textnormal{[PREDICTION — untested as a boundary]}: at the same $(m,r,N)$
  the Adam capacity line is one unit \emph{higher} than SGD's,
  $k\le m+r-N$ against $k\le m+r-N-1$; each cap is already attained in its
  own regime in the table of \S\ref{sec:counting}. So
  identifiability survives; what is lost is the cheap $rN$ reduction, and at
  the $n=96$ work point the Adam solution is $\sim\!400\times$ worse
  conditioned than SGD's ($\kappa=8\times10^5$ vs $2\times10^3$) with a much
  smaller basin. Adam defends by difficulty, not by non-identifiability.
\item \textbf{One Jacobian, three readings.} $DF$ at the truth is a single
  object: its \emph{rank} is identifiability (Theorem~\ref{thm:H}); its
  \emph{spectrum} is the local resolution ellipsoid
  $\{h : h^{\!\top}(J^{\!\top}\Sigma^{-1}J)h<\tau\}$ — the anisotropic set of
  image changes the release cannot distinguish; its \emph{whitened} form is
  detectability, $d^2=2\,\mathrm{KL}$. The valley-width experiments of the
  dataset-sensitivity programme are one-dimensional slices of this ellipsoid.
  Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum} shows the spectrum at the truth for three cells
  \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} (job 479684): full column rank in every case (the
  identifiability reading), the per-block spectra sitting at their caps (the
  counting reading), and the Adam spread — $\kappa\approx8\times10^{5}$
  against SGD's $2\times10^{3}$ at the same shape — as the conditioning
  reading. Rendering the leading singular directions as image-space edits is
  the natural next object \textnormal{[EMPIRICAL]}.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_spectrum.png}
\caption{Singular spectrum of $DF$ at the true $(w,X)$ (black), with the
$B$-block (dashed) and $A$-block (dotted) spectra, for SGD and Adam at the
$n=96$ work point and for the small SGD cell. All three are full column rank

\item \textbf{What actually stops the attack today is neither boundary.}
  Within the capacity region the basin of attraction from perturbed-truth
  starts exceeds a $65\%$ start error with no edge found
  \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]} (job 459111), but starts an attacker can build from
  the release alone (random, span, certificate, span-anchor) reach the floor
  in $1$ of $20$ runs \textnormal{[VERIFIED†]}. The binding constraint is the
  initialiser — the slot the framework reserves for a learned or population
  prior — not information.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What to say to Gal (replaces the lead of Rev~9 \S16)}
\label{sec:gal}

Lead with the cancellation, then the count, then the theorem, then the honest
open item — all four are mathematics.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{The object.} $C=P_{\mathrm{row}(B_T)^\perp}A_T=c_TP_{(A_0H)^\perp}A_0$,
  $CH=0$, $\operatorname{rank}C=r-N$: two released views of one private span;
  projecting one with the subspace the other reveals cancels the seed and
  leaves exactly $r-N$ attacker-visible equations.
\item \emph{The same insight, forward.} The release depends on the seed only
  through $X=A_0U$, $rN$ numbers (Theorem~\ref{thm:G}). Solve for them.
\item \emph{The count.} The release has at most $N(m+r-N)+rN$ degrees of
  freedom against $Nk+rN$ unknowns, so the exact channel's capacity is
  $k<m+r-N$ — $m$ above the quotient's. It is a dimension count whose strict
  form is a theorem (the softmax head costs one per image), it is measured
  sharp to one unit of $k$, and past it — only past it — the release is
  reproduced at the floor by a point that is not the truth. Say in the same
  breath what that point is: within $0.2$--$1.2\%$ of the true images where
  the solver lands, and a continuation along the release-consistent set
  reaches $1.4$--$3.5\%$ along a single null direction — a lower bound on the
  extent, one cell still rising. Non-isolation is established; on the paths
  measured the alternative solutions are still recognisable reconstructions;
  whether the fibre reaches unrecognisable points is not established. The
  line marks where exact recovery stops — that much is a theorem — and what
  it means for recognisable recovery is open. This is a boundary
  of exact identifiability and, on this evidence, not a boundary of leakage —
  the more interesting statement, and the honest one.
  ``$r-N$'' bounds the quotient, not the leakage.
\item \emph{The theorem.} Below the line, $DF$ is full column rank almost
  everywhere (analytic-subvariety dichotomy plus one explicit $T=1$ witness);
  the true data is locally isolated for a.e.\ seed and a.e.\ data set.
\item \emph{What is open, said in the same breath.} Global uniqueness; the
  recipe as parameters (and whether it is self-detecting); how far the fibre
  past the line extends; Adam, which keeps identifiability, loses the $rN$
  reduction, destroys the algebraic channel entirely and — by breaking the
  simplex constraint — hands the attacker one unit of capacity back (a
  prediction); and the fact that what stops the attack today is the
  initialiser, not the information.
\end{enumerate}
